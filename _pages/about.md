Hi! I’m Zahra, a curious mind who enjoys solving real-world problems with creativity. I’m currently a Master’s student and researcher at Mila (Quebec AI Institute) and McGill University, where I work on generative modeling, computer vision, and vision–language foundation models under the supervision of Professor Tal Arbel.

Alongside my academic work, I am a research intern at Mila, working on applied multimodal learning problems. My internship focuses on combining high-resolution geospatial imagery with structured data to support real-world decision-making tasks. In this role, I have built end-to-end multimodal pipelines, explored representation learning across multiple spatial resolutions, and translated research ideas into reproducible, production-oriented systems.

During my Master’s, my research has focused on building and scaling high-resolution diffusion models for medical image generation. I have led projects on ultra-high-resolution image synthesis, scaling from 512×512 up to 2048×2048, using state-of-the-art techniques such as Stable Diffusion XL, progressive upscaling, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and memory-efficient training pipelines for large vision models. This work was published at the DGM4MICCAI 2025 workshop.

I am particularly interested in understanding how these models work internally. I led a project on controllable and interpretable generative modeling, where I studied the structure of Stable Diffusion’s latent space and used language guidance to identify and traverse meaningful latent directions for factorized image generation. This resulted in a first-author paper at MIV @ CVPR 2025.

I have also explored and systematically compared different fine-tuning strategies for adapting large foundation models to the medical imaging domain. This work was published at the ELAMI workshop at MICCAI 2025, and I also wrote an accompanying blog post that won third place in the MICCAI Educational Challenge.

In addition, during my Master’s I co-authored a work on reinforcement learning for prompt–image alignment in medical image generation. I also co-authored a project on leveraging knowledge graphs for diverse and structured medical image synthesis, developed in collaboration with researchers at Stanford University.

I received my Bachelor of Science in Computer Science from Sharif University of Technology. During this time, I completed a research internship at the VITA Lab at EPFL, where I worked on modeling uncertainty in human pose prediction. As part of a team of four, we developed a deep learning library that integrates state-of-the-art pose prediction models, datasets, and evaluation metrics. I also worked on a food computing research project through a collaboration between researchers at my university and the University of California, Irvine, gaining hands-on experience with graph neural networks, transformers, and natural language processing by implementing complex neural network models from scratch. For my undergraduate thesis, I worked on a Virtual Try-On project under the supervision of Professor Mostafa Kamali Tabrizi, where I studied GANs, conditional GANs, teacher–student distillation, human pose estimation, inpainting, and optical flow.

Outside of research, I enjoy journaling and calligraphy. They give me space to reflect and observe the world from a clearer lens. I enjoy long walks with a good podcast and, occasionally, watching a good sci-fi movie.
